01 · Requirements

The idea

Structures deform under load in ways that are hard to predict from the outside, and by the time damage is visible you've usually missed the interesting part. AEFLES instruments the structure directly: embedded strain sensors report how it's actually flexing, in real time, while it's loaded.

The value is in the word real-time. A post-test inspection tells you where a structure ended up. A live strain readout tells you how it got there — which load path carried the force, which region responded first, and whether the structure is behaving the way the model said it would.

03 · Construction

What I built

The interesting engineering problem sat between hardware and software. Strain signals are small, and the difference between a real structural response and an artifact of how a sensor was mounted is not obvious from the data alone. Having a simulation to compare against is what makes the measurement trustworthy — and having real measurements is what keeps the simulation honest. Neither half is worth much without the other, so I built them together rather than bolting simulation on after the sensors were already in place.

Sensor placement was treated as a design decision, not an installation detail — where a sensor goes determines what you're capable of learning, so it had to be decided against the model, not just against convenience.

04 · How I fixed it

Telling a real signal from a mounting artifact

The first surprising result out of a new sensor is far more likely to be a mounting problem than a discovery, so the instrument had to be validated before I trusted anything it reported. That meant checking every surprising reading against the simulation first: if the model and the sensor disagreed, the question wasn't automatically "what did we discover" — it was "which one of these is wrong, and why."

Model and measurement turned out to be a loop, not a one-way check. They correct each other, and the disagreements between them are where the actual learning happens.

05 · Result

What success looked like

A working real-time strain sensing system, with embedded sensors and simulation validating each other closely enough to trust the readings — and a funded proposal turned into an actual build and schedule, not just a plan on paper.
