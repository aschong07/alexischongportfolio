Picking a major is hard when you can't see what it actually looks like. UH Pathfinder matches UH Mānoa students to a path that fits them, then shows them exactly what living it would be like — the classes, the buildings, and a real campus map.

The problem

Most students choose a major from a course catalog and a vague sense of the job it leads to — not from anything that actually shows them what the next four years look like. UH Pathfinder was built to close that gap: sign in, get matched to a major that fits you, and see it made concrete instead of abstract.

What it does

Match

Find your path

Students sign in and get pointed toward a UH Mānoa major that actually fits how they think and what they want out of school.

Preview

See the schooling

Once matched, the app lays out what that major's schooling actually looks like — the classes you'd take and the shape of the path.

Locate

Walk the campus map

An interactive campus map shows where those classes actually happen — which buildings, which schools — so the major stops being abstract.

What I built

I mostly owned the frontend: integrating the UI into a working front end, and shaping the overall look and feel so the app reads as one coherent product rather than a stitched-together prototype. The campus map — showing where each school's buildings actually sit — was one of the pieces I focused on, since it's the part that turns "here's a major" into "here's what your day would look like."

  • A finder is only useful if it's specific.Naming a major means little; showing the buildings and classes attached to it is what makes the match feel real.
  • UI cohesion is its own kind of engineering.Wiring the frontend together and keeping the "vibe" consistent across screens took as much attention as any single feature.
  • Hackathon scope is a design constraint.Getting a sign-in flow, a matching flow, and an interactive map all working together in a short window meant being deliberate about what shipped.
