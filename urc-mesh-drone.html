01 · Requirements

What the system needs to do

The rover, a new drone, and the ground station all need to reach each other, which is a different problem than a single point-to-point link. And a drone adds a constraint the rover doesn't have: everything it carries — radio, compute, camera payload — has to fly on a battery budget the airframe can actually sustain.

02 · Design process

Choosing a mesh over point-to-point

Rather than a single direct link from each node to every other node, I'm implementing a mesh radio network so that any node can relay for another — the drone can extend the rover's range back to the ground station, or vice versa. On the airframe side, the drone is built around a Pixhawk / Orange Cube flight controller, chosen for the same reason most of my hardware choices get made: it's a well-supported, well-documented platform rather than something I'd have to fight to integrate.

03 · Construction

What's built so far

The airframe is assembled around the Pixhawk/Orange Cube flight controller, and the mesh network implementation connecting the rover, drone, and ground station is underway. Alongside that, two earlier-stage threads are growing out of this work: expanding the rover's camera coverage to gather enough footage to train a locally-run autonomous driving model, and prototyping an LLM-based anomaly detector that looks at the drone's imagery and flags what looks wrong.

04 · How I'm fixing it

The current constraint: power budget

Right now the main problem I'm working through is power consumption — figuring out what the airframe can actually carry and sustain once the radio, compute, and camera payload are all drawing from the same battery budget. That's an airframe-first constraint: the fix isn't just a more efficient radio or a lighter camera, it's finding the actual ceiling the airframe can lift and sizing everything else against it, rather than picking components first and hoping they fit.

In progress
